## 2. Core beliefs

- **Price discounts everything.** "The price movement represents the aggregate knowledge of Wall Street and, above all, its aggregate knowledge of coming events" (H I). Adding volume or commodity indexes is "unnecessary" (H I, V).
- **The barometer forecasts.** "The market is not saying what the condition of business is to-day. It is saying what that condition will be months ahead" (H V). Proof cases: the bear market of 1917 and the line of 1914 (H XV, XVII).
- **There are three simultaneous movements.**
  - Primary: "from a year to three years".
  - Secondary: "from a few days to many weeks" (H XIV).
  - Daily: "largely negligible" (H VII).
  - Dow's own estimate of 4–6-year primaries was "far too long" (H III).
- **The averages must agree.** "there is never a primary movement, rarely a secondary movement, where they do not agree" (H I).
- **Trend means the sequence of highs and lows.** "It is a bull period as long as the average of one high point exceeds that of previous high points" (Dow, N VIII; H IV).
- **Values rule in the long run.** "The best way of reading the market is to read from the standpoint of values" (Dow, N IX). Bull markets end "discounting possibilities only" (H IV).
- **Excess at the end.** "Every primary market, bull or bear, tends to overrun itself" (H VIII).
- **Manipulation cannot make a primary.** Hamilton finds it "impossible to recall a single instance of a major market movement which depended for its impetus… upon manipulation" (H VI). Manipulation affects only secondaries, daily moves and single stocks (H VIII).
- **No cycles and no symmetry.** "so far as the price movement is concerned action and reaction are not equal" (H XI). Dow half-believed in a crisis cycle: "five or six years for public confidence to go from the point of too little hope to the point of too much confidence" (N XIX).
- **The crowd is late.** "The speculative public always buys on advances and seldom on declines" (Dow, N XXX). "News known is news discounted" (H VII).

## 3. Explicit decision rules

### 3a. When a signal EXISTS (trend confirmation and entry)

1. **Primary bull (H XV).** After a bear market, a secondary decline "does not decline to the old low figures", and then the market "recovers to points better than the new high established on the earlier rally". That establishes "a primary bull market of indefinite length".
   - Both averages must do it. In Oct 1921 Hamilton required "the repeated new high for both averages" (H VII).
2. **Primary bear (Dow).** "It is a bear period when the low point becomes lower than the previous low points" (N VIII).
3. **Line breakout.** A line is "a succession of closing prices, over a sufficient number of days for a fair volume of trading within a narrow range" (H XIV). A break out of it signals a move "of at least a secondary and even a primary character… where either average is confirmed by the other".
   - **Size:** 1914 ran 66–71 trading days with a 3–4 point range (H XV). Other examples: 70–74 and 73–77 "for a number of weeks" (H I), and "three points or so" (H XXI). That is roughly 3–6% at the index levels of the time.
4. **Resumption.** A line of accumulation after a reaction, followed by a new high above where the reaction began, is "conclusive evidence… that the bull movement had been resumed" (H XIII).
5. **Confirmation timing.** The averages "may not break out of their respective lines on the same day or in the same week" (H XIV). The joint lows or highs mark the turn. A later extreme by one average alone is "negligible" (H XIV).
6. **Entry within the trend (Dow).**
   - "If the main move is up, relapses are speculators' opportunities" (N VIII).
   - Begin buying "when a stock has had four or five points decline from the last previous top". In a bear, sell after a "three or four points rally" (N VI).
   - With a 5-point swing, "it is in the highest degree desirable not to buy when three of these points have passed" (N VII).
   - After a 10-point advance, "a normal setback would be four points" (N XIV).
   - Hamilton: buy "in the dulness after a typical reaction in a bull market" (H XXII).
7. **Size of a secondary.** It is "at least three-eighths of the primary movement" (Dow, N IX; H IV).
8. **Dullness follows the primary.** "In a bull market, dullness is generally followed by advances; in a bear market, by decline" (N XXXV). "Never sell a dull market" is "bad advice in a major bear swing" (H XIII).

### 3b. Exits

- **Primary reversal.** A confirmed lower low, or a break below a line of distribution, in both averages (H X, XIV). Caution: "there is often no marked warning line of distribution at the top of a major bull swing" (H X).
- **Scaling with the trend.**
  - "buy what you can afford when the upward movement is fairly assured, expand your bull account on the profits". Then keep "selling in periods of marked strength", and add back in reactions (H XXI).
  - Exit when the crowd arrives: "when the elevator boy and the shoeblack are asking for bull tips" (H IX).
- **Stops (Dow).**
  - "two points is the wisest place to stop a loss" (N XIII), or "two or three points from the purchase price" (N XI). On ~$100 stocks that is about 2–3%.
  - Trail "about 3 points behind the high prices on the advance" (N XIII), tightening to 2 points on "toppiness" (N XI).
  - Counterweight: for a value-based holder "in harmony with the main tendency… A stop order is folly" (N X). "Losses should not generally be taken on the long side in a bull period" (N VIII).

### 3c. When there is NO signal, so stay out (emphasised by both)

1. **Only one average.** "One of the shortest ways of going wrong is to accept an indication by one average which has not been clearly confirmed by the other" (H XII).
   - Example: in May 1921 the industrials broke up alone and then fell 13 more points.
   - A rails break of "a fraction of a point" did "not yet jointly indicate" a bear resumption (H VII).
2. **Inside a line.** "While the market remains within narrow limits stop orders are a needless waste of money… As long as strength and weakness seem to about balance each other, the chances are for short turns" (N XXXV). A one-day poke "taken back the following day" is not a break (H XV).
3. **Secondaries.**
   - It is "still harder to say when a secondary movement is due" (H XIII). Their prediction is "a chancy sort of business and is not here encouraged" (H XXI).
   - Shorting in a bull is "merely guessing at the secondary reactions" (H XXII).
   - Lines appear "not before but after a secondary break or rally" (H XIII).
4. **Transition periods.**
   - "No knowledge of the stock market barometer will enable any of us to call the absolute turn". Instead, "There may be weeks of narrow fluctuations before a definite trend is established", and these "eat up the speculator's capital" (H XXII).
   - Change is assertable only "weeks or even months afterwards" (H IX). The WSJ conceded the 1907 bear market about two months late (H X).
   - "Slack water" (unsigned, N XXXV): "a period when it is impossible to say definitely that conditions have changed".
5. **Dwindling, quiet regimes** (1910–14). The barometer "stops talking when he has nothing to say" (H XX).
6. **An impaired barometer.**
   - With rails government-guaranteed, "the averages had half their usual value as a barometer, or indeed less than half" (H XVI).
   - Surtax selling "bears no reference to coming events" (H XXI).
   - The market cannot foresee shocks such as the 1906 earthquake (H X).
7. **Single days and single stocks.** "no inference of value can be drawn from a single day's trading" (H XV). "the average is deceptive for speculation in individual stocks" (H I).
8. **Doubt.** "When in doubt do nothing. Don't enter the market on half conviction" (N XXVII, *Financial Record*).

### 3d. Volume rules (secondary evidence only)

- **Bull markets carry more volume.** "It expands as prices go up and contracts as they decline" (H XII). True of primaries, but "subject to great modification in the secondary movements".
- **But volume is weak evidence.** It "is much less significant than is generally supposed. It is purely relative" (H XV).
- **Activity and range.** "Great activity means great movements" (N XXXI).
- **Tipster's rules (low authority, N XXXIV).** It is a bull market when the largest volumes are on advances and "trading falls off on the reactions". Campaigns "culminate in large volumes".

## 4. Testable hypotheses on DAILY OHLCV (my operationalizations)

**Common definitions (mine):**

- **Data.** A = industrials (^DJI or DIA; check with SPY). B = transports (^DJT or IYT). Use **closes only**. Volume is normalised as V/V50.
- **Swing.** A zig-zag on closes with threshold θ (5%; grid 3/5/8%).
- **Secondary.** A counter-trend leg that retraces at least r of the prior leg (3/8; grid 1/3–1/2) and lasts at least d days (10; grid 5–15). Legs past about 60 days are flagged as possible primaries.
- **Line.** N ≥ 15 days (grid 15/20/40/60) with (max C − min C)/mean C ≤ w (grid 3–6%). A break is a close beyond the extreme by ≥ 0.5%, or 2 consecutive closes beyond it.
- **Confirmation window.** W trading days (grid 10–60). Hamilton's cases run from about 7 days (1914) to about 3 months (1909).
- **Execution.** Next open, 5–10 bps cost per side, compared against base rates.

**Hypotheses:**

- **H-DOW-1 — Bull confirmation (H XV).**
  - Rule: for each average, after the primary low L0, find a rally high H1 and a secondary low L1 > L0, then a close > H1. Signal when both trigger within W.
  - Measure: 63/126/252-day forward returns, and the lag from the ex-post low.
  - Variant: Dow's "higher highs".
- **H-DOW-2 — Bear confirmation (N VIII).** The mirror rule: a lower high, then a close below the reaction low, in both averages. Measure forward returns and the false-bear rate (a new BULL within 60 days).
- **H-DOW-3 — Persistence (H X).** Expect P(126-day forward return > 0 | BULL) > base rate > P(… | BEAR). Compare state durations with Hamilton's 1900–23 averages of 25 months (bull) and 15 months (bear) (H V).
- **H-DOW-4 — Non-confirmation = no signal (H XII).**
  - Rule: label closes of A beyond its last secondary extreme as confirmed or unconfirmed by B within W, and vice versa.
  - Expect unconfirmed breaks to show lower 20/60-day continuation and a higher failure rate (back through the level within 20 days).
- **H-DOW-5 — Line breakout (H XIV, XV).** A break confirmed by both averages predicts 20/60-day returns in the break direction. Expect the effect to grow with N (a line gains "significance as the number of trading days increases", H VII) and to disappear for breaks in one average only.
- **H-DOW-6 — Post-secondary lines (H XIII).**
  - In BULL, measure P(close above the pre-reaction high within 60 days | up-break of a line formed after a secondary low).
  - Also count lines that precede versus follow the start of a secondary. Expect "follow" to dominate.
- **H-DOW-7 — Anatomy of secondaries (N IX; H V, XIII).**
  - Retracement: at least 3/8 (median about 1/3–2/3).
  - Duration: mostly under 40 days.
  - Asymmetry: in BULL, recovery time > decline time ("short and sharp, with a halting recovery", H V).
- **H-DOW-8 — Do not fade the primary (H XIII, XXI, XXII).**
  - Test: in BULL, counter-trend shorts (5-day RSI > 80, double top, downside line break) should have expectancy ≤ 0 net of costs. Run long entries on the same bars for comparison.
  - Also fit a classifier for "a secondary starts within 10 days". Expect AUC ≈ 0.5.
- **H-DOW-9 — Volume (H XII, XV).**
  - (a) Up-day V/V50 > down-day V/V50 in BULL, and the reverse in BEAR.
  - (b) Volume adds only marginal out-of-sample lift to H-DOW-1/5.
  - (c, low authority, N XXXIV) A 3-day advance at ≥ 2×V50 late in a bull marks a short-term top.
- **H-DOW-10 — Panic anatomy (WSJ 1906 in H X; N XXXI, XXXV).**
  - Define a panic as a fall of at least 7% from the 20-day high within 5 days. Nelson's panics averaged 7–10%. Grid: 6–10%.
  - Predictions:
    - The low comes by day 2–3.
    - The rebound is 40–60% of the decline, or "more than half", within 5–30 days.
    - An "irregular sag" then gives back at least half the rebound. In a bear market, "all the recovery and more is lost".
  - Trade implication: buy the retest, not the first rally.
- **H-DOW-11 — Dullness resolves with the primary (N XXXV).** Define dull as 20-day realised volatility in the bottom quintile of its 1-year range and V20/V50 < 0.8, starting at least 10 days after a secondary extreme. Expect the sign of the 20/60-day forward return to match the BULL/BEAR state.
- **H-DOW-12 — Buy reactions, not extensions (N VI, VII, XIV).** In BULL, compare entries after a ≥ 4% pullback from the swing high with entries after more than 60% of a typical swing has already passed. Expect the former to show better forward returns and smaller MAE.
- **H-DOW-13 — Dow's stops (N X–XIV).** On H-DOW-12 entries, compare three arms:
  - (a) a 2% initial stop plus a 3% trail from the highest close, tightened to 2% after +10%;
  - (b) no stop, exit on BEAR;
  - (c) a 2.5% trail.
  - The text supports (a) for "point" traders and (b) for with-trend holders.
- **H-DOW-14 — Transition chop is costly (H XXII; N XXXV).** Run generic 20-day breakouts only between a reversal warning (an unconfirmed lower low or line break) and confirmation. Expect expectancy ≤ 0 net of costs, against > 0 inside confirmed states.
- **H-DOW-15 — Impaired barometer (H XVI, XXI; my operationalization).** Suppress signals when the 120-day correlation of A and B is < 0.3, or when B tracks Treasuries (TLT) more than A. Expect lower hit-rates in these windows.
- **H-DOW-16 — Double and triple tops (H IV, XIV; N IX, XXXV).** Define a second top within ±1.5%, at least 20 days apart, with an intervening drop of at least 5%, followed by a break of the intervening low. The authors are ambivalent ("by no means infallible, but is often useful"; Dow: "a good many times when signals are not given"). Expect a weaker edge than H-DOW-2.

## 5. Caveats the authors admit

- **It is not a system.** "it is not a method of beating the speculative market — not an advertised system of stock trading, guaranteed against loss" (H XX). Nelson: "No infallible plan has yet been discovered" (Preface).
- **No exact turns and no duration.** "It would be absurd to say that it was possible to call the exact turn in the major swings" (H X). It is "impossible for the barometer to predict the duration of the movement" (H XV). Lag runs weeks to months (H IX).
- **Errors come "mainly"** from "the almost impossibility of forecasting the secondary movement" (H X).
- **Single stocks.** The theory is "valueless for individual stocks, except that these do not commonly make serious advances except when the general trend of the market is upwards" (H XXI).
- **Thin evidence.** "Our table of averages is only truly effective for rather over a quarter of a century" (H XIV). Dow's own duration estimates were wrong (H III).
- **The books disagree on bull versus bear length.** N XXIX and XXXV say bears last longer. Hamilton's count says bulls average 25 months and bears 15 (H V).
- **Shocks and structural breaks** distort the barometer: earthquake, war, nationalised rails, taxes (H X, XVI, XXI).
- **Reader bias.** "They become deceptive if and when the wish is father to the thought" (H XII). "Don't let us overwork it" (H XVI).
- **My caveat.** Hamilton's record ("no instance of a radical misinterpretation", H X) is self-graded and ex post. Phrases like "sufficient number of days" are judgment calls, so fix the parameters out-of-sample.

## 6. Verdict for the rulebook author (10 lines)

1. Dow theory is an index-level regime classifier (long, short or flat), not a timing system.
2. The core signal is a higher secondary low plus a break of the prior rally high, in both averages (H-DOW-1/2).
3. NO-SIGNAL #1: non-confirmation between averages. One average alone is not a signal (H-DOW-4).
4. NO-SIGNAL #2: inside a line (about 3–6% range for weeks). Wait for a confirmed close outside it, and ignore one-day pokes (H-DOW-5).
5. NO-SIGNAL #3: never trade secondaries against the primary. The authors call them unforecastable (H-DOW-8).
6. NO-SIGNAL #4: transitions, "slack water" and dwindling, quiet regimes are explicitly unreadable (H-DOW-14).
7. Volume is supporting evidence only. Hamilton is near-dismissive, so test what it adds (H-DOW-9).
8. Inside a confirmed trend, enter on reactions of about 3/8 of the prior leg or in the dullness after them, never on extensions (H-DOW-11/12).
9. Expect lag. The edge, if any, is persistence, not catching turns (H-DOW-3).
10. For single stocks, use Dow only as a market-regime gate.
